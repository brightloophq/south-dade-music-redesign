# 07 — THE WALK

### Creative direction, v2 — the homepage that deserves to be featured

**Date:** 2026-08-06
**Supersedes:** `01-homepage-blueprint.md` §3 (section order) and everything built in Phase 5B
**Status:** Creative direction. No implementation.

---

## 0. The diagnosis

The homepage we shipped is **technically correct and emotionally inert.** I built
it, so let me be exact about why.

**It is a legal brief, not an experience.** Nine sections, each retiring one
documented objection. Claim, evidence, claim, evidence. That structure is correct
for a landing page and fatal for a feeling. Nothing on it *happens to you.*

**The hero is a poster.** "The Bill" is a smart reference *to* theatre. It is not
an experience *of* theatre. A poster on a wall has never made anyone cry. The
curtain going up has.

**The signature moment is an infographic.** A dot travelling a rule is a
progress bar with mood lighting. We applied abstraction to the least abstract
thing imaginable: a frightened child walking into light.

**Three fatal omissions:**

| Missing | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Sound** | A music school website with no audio. Five phases, nobody said it. This is the single largest missed opportunity in the project — and here it is not a gimmick, it *is* the product. |
| **A body** | There is no person on the page. We treated the consent gate as an obstacle to route around instead of asking what it was trying to tell us. |
| **A moment** | The page describes ninety days. It never *delivers* the twelve seconds that ninety days exist to produce. |

---

## 1. The reframe

We have been selling the wrong noun.

Not music lessons. Not confidence. Not even a showcase.

> ## The product is the twelve seconds before a nine-year-old plays her first note in front of strangers.

That is the thing the parent is buying. That is what they replay in the car
afterwards. Everything else — the weeks, the guarantee, the tuition — is
logistics attached to that moment.

**So the homepage should be that moment.** Not a description of it. Not a
diagram of it. The moment itself, stretched to the length of a scroll.

---

## 2. THE WALK

> **The homepage is the walk from the wings to the microphone, in the dark.**
>
> **The scroll is the walk. You are the child.**

You arrive backstage. Black. You hear a room through a wall — muffled, full of
people. You start to move, and the scroll becomes footsteps on a wooden stage.
Light grows from one edge. You reach your mark. Everything stops. And then a
note.

That is the entire page. There are no sections. There is a building, and you
walk through it.

### Why this earns the ambition

Most award-bait sites impose a cinematic metaphor on a business that has nothing
to do with it. **This business's core experience literally is this walk.** The
90-Day Stage Program exists to produce it. The Gradual Exposure Ladder is a
method for surviving it. The guarantee is a promise about it.

We are not dressing a music school as a film. We are finally showing the thing
the school actually sells.

### Reference frame

| | What we take |
|---|---|
| **A24** | Withholding. Let the frame be dark. Trust the viewer. Never explain the shot. |
| **Apple** | Scroll as a directed camera; obsessive restraint; one idea per screen. |
| **Nike** | Physical POV. You are inside the body, not watching it. |
| **MasterClass** | Cinematic top, conventional close. Sell the feeling, transact in the light. |
| **Broadway** | The house, the hush, the note. |

**Anti-references:** every scroll-jacked agency site with a custom cursor and a
loading percentage. We are not doing craft-as-decoration. Every device below is
load-bearing.

---

## 3. The unlock: you never see a face

Gate I-1 blocks all 18 photographs for lack of consent. We treated that as an
obstacle. It was a brief.

> ## Nobody's face appears on this homepage. Ever. That is the concept, not the compromise.

You see: the **back** of a small head in light. A **silhouette** against a lit
scrim. **Hands** on keys. A **shoulder** in the wings. The audience as **dark
shapes**. A parent's **hands** in a lap. The back of a neck. A shadow crossing
a floor.

**Four reasons this is stronger than faces:**

1. **You become her.** A face is someone else's child. A back is your own.
2. **It's universal.** No age, no gender, no race is specified — so every family
   in South Miami-Dade sees theirs.
3. **It is how cinema builds dread and release.** Withholding the face is the
   oldest and best trick in the book. The reveal you never get is the one you
   supply yourself.
4. **It is ethically bulletproof.** Silhouettes and backs sidestep the entire
   consent problem — and we obtain consent anyway, because it is right.

**This is the single most important decision in this document.** It converts the
project's biggest blocker into its signature. The shoot can happen next week.

### The shot list this implies

```
· Back of a head, small, walking away into light
· Silhouette in a doorway, warm rim light on one shoulder
· Hands on piano keys — tight, shallow, one hand trembling slightly
· Wooden floor, scuffed, one shaft of light across it
· The back of a child's neck, the shape of an audience beyond, thrown out of focus
· Dark shapes of heads in rows — an audience from the stage
· A parent's hands in a lap, gripping a folded programme
· An empty chair on a stage, lit
· A curtain edge, still
· Feet at a taped mark on the floor
```

Ten frames. Not one of them requires a face, a release, or a named child. Every
one of them is more beautiful than what is on the site now.

## 4. Sound

> **Nothing on this page will be remembered as vividly as the sound.**

This is where the site becomes unforgettable, and it is the one thing no
competitor will copy because it requires taste.

### The bed

| Act | Sound |
|---|---|
| **Backstage** | Room tone through a wall. Muffled voices, a chair scraping, a distant tuning note. Cloth. A breath. |
| **The walk** | **Footsteps on a wooden stage, one per scroll increment.** The room gets less muffled with every step. |
| **The mark** | **Everything drops out.** One high, thin room tone. Near silence. |
| **The note** | A single piano note. Clean, close, loud. No reverb tail at first — then the room arrives. |
| **After** | Applause, but *wrong on purpose*: distant, warm, slightly muffled, like you're hearing it from the stage with blood in your ears. Then one voice, close: a parent saying *"that's my girl"* under their breath. |
| **Houselights** | Silence. Room tone returns, ordinary now. |

### Rules

- **Off by default.** Always. A site that makes noise at a stranger is a site
  that gets closed.
- **The invitation is designed, not a browser default.** Bottom-left, small,
  permanent: `◦ sound off — this was made to be heard`. One tap.
- **The page is complete in silence.** Every emotional beat has a visual
  equivalent. Sound doubles it; it never carries it alone.
- **One control, always reachable.** Never a modal. Never autoplay. Never a
  "click anywhere to begin."
- **Ducking:** if the visitor scrolls fast, the bed thins rather than
  scrambling. Speed is respected, not punished.

### ACT II — THE WALK · 100–380vh

**The mechanic that makes this site unforgettable.**

The viewport is pinned. Scroll no longer moves a page — it moves **you**, one
footstep at a time.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ letterbox ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ │
│                                                                    │
│                                                    ░░░░░░░         │
│                                              ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░        │
│         she has been practising                ░░░░░░░░░░░░░       │
│         this for eleven weeks                    ░░░░░░░░░░        │
│                                                                    │
│      ────────────────────────────────────────────────────          │
│           the floor, moving toward you, one step at a time         │
│ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ letterbox ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Element | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Letterbox** | Opens at 2.39:1. Black bars, top and bottom. It is a film until we say otherwise. |
| **The floor** | A single photographic plate of a scuffed wooden stage floor, moving toward camera. Not a loop — a long plate, scrubbed. |
| **The camera** | A 6px vertical bob, once per step, eased. Below conscious notice; felt in the stomach. |
| **The light** | One warm source, offscreen right, growing from 4px to filling the right third. Everything in the scene is lit by it and responds to it. |
| **The type** | The parent's thoughts, not the school's copy. 20px. Appearing and dissolving in the periphery, never centred, never more than eight words. |
| **Sound** | One footstep per increment. The room de-muffles as you approach. |

**In-frame lines, in order** (each appears alone, then dissolves):

```
she has been practising this for eleven weeks

you can hear the room from here

three hundred people, or maybe forty — it doesn't matter

week one, she wouldn't put her hand up in class
```

> ▸ **Why the copy is in second person about someone else.** "She" keeps the
> child universal. "You can hear" puts you in the body. The grammar does the
> casting.

**The scroll-jacking question, answered honestly.** This *is* a pin, and pinning
is easy to abuse. The rules that keep it legitimate: normal scroll speed always
exits · no snapping · no wheel hijacking · a flick to the bottom works · a
permanent `skip the film ↓` in the corner from the first frame. **The user is
never trapped. They are invited.**

### ACT III — THE MARK · 380–450vh

**The boldest move on the page: nothing happens.**

You arrive at a taped mark on the floor. Scroll continues to advance the
timeline, but for roughly 1.5 seconds of scrolling there is **no movement, no
new type, and no sound at all.** The bed cuts. Not fades — cuts.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ │
│                                                                    │
│                        ▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄                              │
│                    ▄▄▄▄  the back of a  ▄▄▄▄                       │
│                   ▄▄  small head, in light  ▄▄                     │
│              ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░                  │
│         ░░ dark shapes — rows of them — out of focus ░░            │
│                                                                    │
│                      ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─                               │
│ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The room is revealed for the first time — **from behind her.** We see what she
sees: her own shoulders, and forty dark shapes.

One line, and it is the only bold statement in the entire first half:

```
                          This is week twelve.
```

> ▸ **Silence is the most expensive thing on a website.** Everyone fills every
> moment. Holding 1.5 seconds of absolute nothing takes more nerve than any
> animation, and it is what makes the next beat land like a slap.

### ACT V — HOW · 500–780vh

**The twelve weeks, told backwards.**

The old page had a syllabus table running week 1 → 12. Forward is a *plan*.
Nobody feels a plan.

**Backwards is a revelation.** You have just watched her play. Now you walk back
down the ladder and find out who she was.

```
   scroll ↓            we travel BACK in time

   WEEK 12   the light you just stood in                    ▓ warm
   WEEK 11   a smaller room. eleven faces. she got through it.   ▓
   WEEK 09   she played the whole piece without stopping     ▓▓
   WEEK 06   the first time she played for one other student  ▓▓▓
   WEEK 03   her teacher's room. just the two of them.        ▓▓▓▓
   WEEK 01   she asked if she could just watch.               ▓▓▓▓▓ cold
```

Each step: the ground cools, the light narrows, the room gets smaller. The
warmth we spent four acts earning drains away, and lands on a child who asked if
she could just watch.

Then, at the bottom, one line — and it is the verbatim FAQ answer that has been
buried in an accordion for two years:

```
                    "What if my child is too shy?"

                 That is exactly what we train for.
```

> ▸ **Reverse chronology is the strongest structural idea in this document after
> the walk itself.** It converts a syllabus into a character study, and it makes
> the method (the Gradual Exposure Ladder) *self-evident* rather than explained.
> You have just watched the ladder in reverse. Nobody needs the diagram.

### ACT VII — THE DOOR · 860–940vh

**Houselights.** The ground lifts from dark to warm white over a full viewport of
scroll. Not a cut — a slow, physical rise, the way a real house comes up.

The interface returns. Type sits down to normal size and weight. The letterbox
is long gone. This is the first plain, bright, ordinary screen on the page, and
after twelve hundred vertical pixels of darkness it feels like **relief and
permission.**

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                    │
│   Come and stand in it.                                            │
│                                                                    │
│   It's a 90-Day Stage Experience Preview. We hold your spot        │
│   for $25, credited to your tuition when you enrol.                │
│                                                                    │
│   [  Book a trial — $25  ]      the next showcase is in ⚠ weeks    │
│                                                                    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**One CTA on the entire page.** Not three. Not a sticky bar shadowing you through
the film — that would be a salesman walking beside you during a funeral. The
page earns the right to ask exactly once.

⚠️ *"The next showcase is in X weeks"* requires gate I-4. Without a real date it
is cut, not faked.

### ACT VIII — HOUSE LIGHTS · footer

Everything practical, in a properly designed, generous, bright footer that is
almost a small sitemap: programmes, all seven instruments, scholarships, camps,

**This is where the cut sections go.** The programme grid and the instrument grid
were killing the spell on the page. In the footer they are exactly right —
useful, scannable, undramatic.

**And here, finally, the wordmark.** First appearance on the entire page.

## 7. Typography

Two families, unchanged. What changes is the **dynamic range.**

> The typographic idea is the distance between a whisper and a shout — and how
> rarely we shout.

| Register | Size | Weight | Where | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Whisper** | 20px | Inter 400 | Every in-frame line, Acts I–III | ~12 times |
| **Statement** | 40px | Archivo 600, width 100 | *"This is week twelve."* · the FAQ answer | 2 times |
| **Shout** | 180px | Archivo 700, width 112 | Act IV only | **once** |
| **Speaking voice** | 17–24px | Inter / Archivo | Acts VII–VIII | throughout |

**One shout per page.** The current build uses `display-xl` in the hero and
`display-md` in four more places. Five shouts is no shouts.

### The device: type that is lit

Type is not white — it is **lit by the same source as the scene.** As the light
moves from the right edge, glyph brightness shifts with it: `n-0` where the
light falls, `n-400` in shadow. Sub-perceptual, and it makes the words feel like
they are **in the room** rather than on the glass.

Nobody will consciously notice. Everybody will feel it.

### Other rules

- **No centred type until Act VII.** The film is hung left, like a subtitle.
- **Never more than eight words per frame** in Acts I–IV.
- **Film grain over every glyph** and every frame — one animated noise layer at
  3% opacity. The cheapest thing on this list and the single biggest contributor
  to "this feels like film."
- Spanish is authored, not translated. *"Aún no sabe que puede hacerlo"* is a
  better line than its English original; the Spanish tree gets its own writer.

```
  #000000  ████  ACT I     true black. absence.
  #0A0D14  ███▓  ACT II    black with the first blue in it
  #0A0D14  ███▓  ACT III   unchanged — the stillness is chromatic too
  #FFF4E0  ░░░░  ACT IV    ▲ THE FLASH — 400ms of warm white
  #14100C  ███▒  ACT V     warm dark. BROWN-black, not blue-black — memory
  #0D1220  ███▓  ACT VI    back to the stage blue. the promise is present tense
  #FAF9F7  ░░░░  ACT VII   houselights. warm white.
  #FAF9F7  ░░░░  ACT VIII  plain. ordinary. competent.
```

**The one new colour decision:** Act V is a **warm brown-black**, not the
system's blue-black. Memory is warmer than the present. Nobody will name it;
everybody will feel that the past is a different temperature from the stage.

**Amber remains rationed to near-nothing.** In four acts of darkness, `spot-500`
appears as: the seam of light, the source in Act II, the flash, and the CTA
fill. **Four times.** That is why it means something.

## 9. Motion concepts

Six devices. Every one is load-bearing; anything decorative was cut.

| # | Device | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Scroll = footsteps** | Converts a UI gesture into a physical act. The whole concept lives here. |
| **2** | **One moving light source** | Every element is lit by it — floor, figure, type, grain. A single unifying physics makes the page feel like a *place*. |
| **3** | **The held breath** (Act III) | 1.5s of absolute stillness and silence. Nerve as a design device. |
| **4** | **The flash cut + letterbox open** (Act IV) | 400ms of warm white; the bars fly open. The film becomes the world. |
| **5** | **Reverse chronology** (Act V) | Scroll travels backward in time. Turns a syllabus into a character study. |
| **6** | **The houselights rise** (Act VII) | A full viewport of slow luminance change. Physical, not a cut. Relief. |

### Explicitly rejected

❌ Custom cursor — cliché, and banned by our own motion system
❌ Preloader with a percentage — **Act I *is* the load screen.** Black with one line of type, buying 1.2s of legitimate loading time while looking like art direction
❌ Horizontal scroll — nauseating on trackpads, hostile on phones
❌ WebGL/3D — nothing here needs it; it would cost the LCP and buy atmosphere we already have
❌ Text scramble, typewriter, magnetic buttons, tilt cards — all of it
❌ Parallax on text — unreadable and an accessibility failure
❌ A sticky CTA during the film — a salesman at a funeral

## 10. Emotional pacing

```
 tension
    ▲
    │                                    ╱▔▔╲  ← ACT IV: the note
    │                              ╱▔▔▔▔╱    ╲
    │                   ╱▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔╱ ACT III    ╲
    │            ╱▔▔▔▔▔╱  the mark             ╲
    │      ╱▔▔▔▔╱ ACT II                        ╲___  ACT V
    │  ╱▔▔╱ ACT I                                   ▔▔▔╲___ VI
    │ ╱                                                    ▔▔▔╲__ VII/VIII
    └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────▶ scroll
      dread    momentum   held breath   RELEASE   understanding   readiness
```

**The shape is a held breath and an exhale.** Four acts of accumulating tension,
one violent release, then a long, warm, decreasing glide into a decision.

**Total: ~1,000vh.** Long. Deliberately. The current page is 850vh of *sections*;
this is 1,000vh of *one continuous thing*, and the first 500vh reads as a
ninety-second film rather than as scrolling.

**The escape hatch is permanent.** `skip the film ↓` from frame one, landing at
Act VII. A parent with fifteen minutes and four tabs gets the price in one tap.
Roughly 40% will take it, and **that is a success, not a failure** — they arrive
at the offer having seen that this place is serious.

## 11. Accessibility — the parallel cut

This business serves Unique Abilities scholarship students. A film that excludes
them is not an award-winner; it is a failure with good lighting.

**Reduced motion gets a genuinely separate design, not a degradation.** Same
story, same copy, same photography, cut as a **photo essay**: ten stills, full
bleed, with the in-frame lines set beneath them. No pin, no scrub, no flash — Act
IV's white frame becomes a static warm-white plate with the same line on it.

It is a beautiful artefact in its own right. Some visitors will prefer it.

| Requirement | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **The flash (Act IV)** | ⚠️ The single biggest risk on the page. 400ms full-viewport luminance change. Must be verified against WCAG 2.3.1; **removed entirely under reduced motion**; never repeats; never triggers on scroll-back |
| **Screen readers** | The page is authored as a short story in semantic HTML. Every line is real text in document order. A screen reader gets a coherent piece of writing with no scroll dependency |
| **Keyboard** | `skip the film` is the first tab stop after the skip link. Every act is an anchor |
| **Sound** | Off by default, one control, never required |
| **Captions** | If the showcase film is ever used, captions are mandatory |
| **Low-end devices** | Below the capability threshold, the photo essay is served — not a broken film |
| **Focus** | Full strength, never animated, including inside the pin |

## 13. Would it deserve to be featured?

**Yes — if three things are true.**

### What earns it

1. **A genuine, defensible concept.** Not "cinematic" as a style; the walk is
   literally the product. Juries can smell a metaphor bolted onto a client.
2. **The face rule.** Turning a legal blocker into the signature is exactly the
   kind of thinking that gets written up.
3. **Sound design as substance**, on a music school, where it is the subject.
4. **The nerve to hold silence** and to open on black with nothing to click.
5. **Reverse chronology** — a structural idea, not an effect.
6. **A reduced-motion cut that is a designed artefact.** Juries increasingly ask.

### What would sink it

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Craft.** The floor plate, the footstep timing, the note, the grain — if any of it is 80%, the whole thing reads as pastiche. There is no partial credit for this concept | Budget the shoot and the sound design as primary line items, not polish |
| **Performance.** A 1,000vh scrubbed film that stutters on a mid-range Android is worse than the page we already have | Photo-essay cut is served below the capability threshold. LCP is a text node on black |
| **The flash.** One WCAG 2.3.1 failure and it is indefensible for this client | Verify with a photosensitivity tool. Removed under reduced motion. This is a hard gate |
| **Conversion.** The parent with four tabs | The escape hatch, and measure it against the current page. **If it converts worse, it loses, no matter what it wins** |

### What this costs

A photographer for one day (ten frames, no faces, no releases blocking). A sound
designer for three days. A motion lead for three weeks. **The build is the
cheapest part.** The current page's cost is in gates; this one's is in craft.
