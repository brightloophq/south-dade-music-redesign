# SOUTH DADE MUSIC
## Design Brief — Homepage

**Florida City, Florida · Prepared 7 August 2026**
**For: Claude Design · Single-document brief**

---

> ## The product is not music lessons.
>
> ## It is the twelve seconds before a nine-year-old plays her first note in front of strangers.

That is the thing the parent is buying. That is what they replay in the car
afterwards. Everything else — the weeks, the guarantee, the tuition — is
logistics attached to that moment.

**The homepage should be that moment.** Not a description of it. Not a diagram
of it. The moment itself, stretched to the length of a scroll.

---

# 1. Business overview

South Dade Music is a music academy at 601 W Palm Dr, Florida City, Florida —
the southern edge of Miami-Dade County, where the suburbs give out and the
Everglades begin. It is not a chain, not a franchise, not a conservatory. It is
a local institution that families reach by car in under twenty minutes.

It teaches eight instruments and voice. It takes students from age three. It
runs a summer camp. And it owns one genuinely distinctive offer: a
ninety-day programme that ends with the student on a stage.

**It serves Florida City, Homestead, Cutler Bay, Palmetto Bay and nearby areas.**
A large proportion of those families speak Spanish at home.

The business has been selling the wrong noun. It sells lessons. What it produces
is nerve.

---

# 2. Audience

## Primary — the parent

A parent considering music education for a child aged roughly 5 to 15.

They are not shopping for musical skill. They are shopping for **evidence that
their child can do a hard thing in front of people.** Music is the vehicle. The
transformation is the purchase.

Their stated objection is price. **Their real objection is:**

> *"My child is too shy for this."*

Everything on this page is an answer to that sentence. The design's job is to
make the answer feel *safe* rather than merely *stated* — because a parent who
believes their child will be humiliated will not book, at any price.

## Secondary — the teenager

A thirteen- to seventeen-year-old who must find this academy **inspiring rather
than childish.**

This audience has veto power. A teenager embarrassed by the website will refuse
the lesson their parent wants to buy. They will not articulate why. They will
simply say no.

**This is the single strongest argument against the primary-colour,
cartoon-instrument, rainbow-confetti idiom that dominates the category** — and
the strongest argument for treating this like a performing-arts institution
rather than a children's activity provider.

## Language

Spanish is not a secondary consideration. It is a large share of the market.

The current site claims *"full bilingual support across our core programs and
enrollment processes."* No Spanish page exists. No language switcher exists.
That claim is currently false, and it is one of the business's open decisions.

**Design as though Spanish will ship.** Spanish runs 15–25% longer than English.
Any type set tight to a measure must survive that expansion. Check the longest
Spanish string at every size where type is set large.

# 3. Brand personality

**Composed. Exact. Warm without sentimentality. Confident without overclaiming.**

The voice of a good programme note: it tells you what is about to happen, it
does not oversell it, and it trusts you to feel something on your own.

| It is | It is not |
|---|---|
| Cinematic | Theatrical in the pantomime sense |
| Serious about children | Serious *at* children |
| Warm | Cute |
| Restrained | Cold |
| Specific | Corporate |
| Local and proud of it | Provincial |

**The single sentence that best demonstrates the register:**

> *That's not shyness. That's a skill she hasn't been taught yet.*

It moves the problem from the child's *character* to a *teachable gap*. That
move is what makes everything else on the page purchasable. Match that tone: one
turn of thought, delivered plainly, no exclamation mark.

**Never:** peppy, keyword-stuffed, emoji-punctuated, or reassuring in the way
that signals there is something to be reassured about.

# 4. Business goals

The current site was audited against the eight things a visitor might arrive
wanting. **Six of the eight are blocked.**

| The visitor wants to… | Today |
|---|---|
| Book a trial lesson | ✅ Works |
| **See a price before committing** | ❌ Blocked — no price published for any lesson or programme |
| **Use a Step Up scholarship** | ❌ Blocked — no eligibility, no steps, no documents, no link, no scholarship CTA |
| **See past performances** | ❌ Blocked — six undated photos; both event pages return errors |
| **Meet the teachers** | ❌ Blocked — not a single instructor is named anywhere on the site |
| **Read it in Spanish** | ❌ Blocked — claimed, not built |
| Ask a question | ⚠️ Degraded — no contact form exists; two different email addresses are published |
| Book the camp | ⚠️ Degraded — generic enquiry form, no seat selection, undisclosed deposit |

Further structural problems the design must solve:

- **One form serves every intent.** Trial bookings, flagship-programme enquiries
  and $450 camp reservations all submit the same external widget. The site's
  most important conversion is indistinguishable from its most casual.
- **Twenty calls to action carry no link at all** — concentrated at the foot of
  the instrument and programme pages, which is the exact point of highest
  purchase intent.
- **Four buttons labelled "Schedule a Trial Lesson" lead to a page with no form
  on it.**
- **The site contains zero forms of its own.** One hundred per cent of lead
  capture depends on a third-party widget.

## The five goals, in priority order

1. **Make a parent feel the transformation before they read a single benefit.**
2. **Make the trial unmissable and unambiguous** — one action, one price, one
   destination, visible at every scroll depth without ever nagging.
3. **Make the flagship programme distinguishable** from a generic trial.
4. **Make the scholarship path real** for the families who need it most.
5. **Make a teenager willing to be seen on this website.**

# 6. The 90-Day Stage Program

The flagship. The reason this business is interesting.

> **A structured twelve-week programme that ends with the student performing in
> a live showcase.**

| Weeks 1–10 | Skill development and rehearsal |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Week 11** | **Peer exposure — they play for the class** |
| **Week 12** | **Dress rehearsal and live showcase** |

Told a second way on the programme page: *Build the Foundation → Practice Like a
Performer → Get Ready for the Stage.*

Underneath it sits a method the business calls the **Gradual Exposure Ladder** —
a staged progression from playing alone, to playing for one person, to playing
for the class, to playing for a room. **This is the answer to "my child is too
shy."** It deserves to be felt on the page, not merely named.

There is also a **Stage-Ready Guarantee**: if a student is not ready, the
coaching continues.

## Three unresolved problems the design must not paper over

1. **Twelve weeks is eighty-four days, not ninety.** The product name, the
   headline claim and the week-by-week breakdown do not reconcile.
2. **The promise is unconditional in two places and conditional in two others.**
   The footer says *every student performs*. The programme page says students
   *get the chance to perform*. This is the business's most serious exposure.
   **Until it is resolved, the words "every student" appear nowhere on the site.**
3. **No tuition is published anywhere** for the flagship offer.

**Design implication:** the page cannot lean on a guarantee it is not allowed to
make. It must create conviction through *atmosphere and structure* — by showing
the shape of the ninety days so clearly that the parent supplies the confidence
themselves.

# 7. The $25 trial offer

The only price on this website that may be published.

> **It is a 90-Day Stage Experience Preview. We hold your spot for $25, which is
> credited to your tuition upon enrollment.**

That is verbatim, and it is an excellent offer — a refundable-by-credit
commitment device, low enough to be impulsive, high enough to filter tyre-kickers.

**It currently appears on two pages out of twenty-eight.** The other
twenty-six carry more than thirty calls to action that say only *"Schedule a
Trial Lesson"* — no price, no terms, no explanation of what happens next.

**This is the largest conversion opportunity on the site.** The $25 and its
credited-back term should be visible wherever the trial is offered, and the act
of booking should feel like reserving a seat at a performance rather than
submitting an enquiry.

# 9. The Walk — the creative concept

**Confirmed. Not up for redesign.** Everything visual must serve it.

> ## The homepage is the walk from the wings to the microphone, in the dark.
>
> ## The scroll is the walk. You are the child.

You arrive backstage. Black. You hear a room through a wall — muffled, full of
people. You start to move, and the scroll becomes footsteps on a wooden stage.
Light grows from one edge. You reach your mark. Everything stops.

And then a note.

**There are no sections. There is a building, and you walk through it.**

## Why this earns the ambition

Most award-bait websites impose a cinematic metaphor on a business that has
nothing to do with cinema. **This business's core experience literally is this
walk.** The 90-Day Stage Program exists to produce it. The Gradual Exposure
Ladder is a method for surviving it. The guarantee is a promise about it.

We are not dressing a music school as a film. We are finally showing the thing
the school actually sells.

## Reference frame

| | What we take |
|---|---|
| **A24** | Withholding. Let the frame be dark. Never explain the shot. |
| **Apple** | Scroll as a directed camera. Obsessive restraint. One idea per screen. |
| **Nike** | Physical point of view. You are inside the body, not watching it. |
| **MasterClass** | Cinematic top, conventional close. Sell the feeling; transact in the light. |
| **Broadway** | The house. The hush. The note. |

**Anti-reference:** every scroll-jacked agency site with a custom cursor and a
loading percentage. Craft here is load-bearing or it is cut.

## The unlock

> ## Nobody's face appears on this homepage. Ever.
> ## That is the concept, not the compromise.

You see the **back** of a small head in light. A **silhouette** against a lit
scrim. **Hands** on keys. A **shoulder** in the wings. The audience as **dark
shapes**. A parent's **hands** in a lap. A **shadow** crossing a floor.

**Four reasons this is stronger than faces:**

1. **You become her.** A face is someone else's child. A back is your own.
2. **It is universal.** No age, gender or race is specified, so every family in
   South Miami-Dade sees theirs.
3. **It is how cinema builds dread and release.** The reveal you never get is
   the one you supply yourself.
4. **It is ethically bulletproof.** It sidesteps a genuine consent problem — and
   consent gets obtained anyway, because it is right.

# 10. Typography direction

**Typography carries this experience.** That is not a stylistic preference. It is
a consequence of section 14: there are almost no usable photographs, and no
video at all.

## The problem to solve first

The current site is set in **Inter**.

The brief for this project lists what the site must not resemble: a SaaS landing
page, a template, a generic school website, an AI-generated startup page. Those
prohibitions describe a single visual dialect — and **Inter is that dialect's
native typeface.** It was drawn, superbly, to be *invisible in software
interfaces.*

Invisibility is the one quality unaffordable here.

Nobody in South Miami-Dade will say *"that's Inter."* They will say **"this
looks like an app."** Same failure, reported by a parent instead of a designer.

## The proposed pairing

**Grotesque for the building. Serif for the person inside it.**

| Role | Face | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | **Archivo** — weight 100–900, **width 62–125** | Wordmark, navigation, labels, data, the week counter, the one shout |
| **Voice** | **Newsreader** — optical size 6–72, weight 200–800, roman and *italic* | Every whispered in-frame line, pull quotes, all reading copy |

**Archivo** reads institutional rather than corporate. It does what a cultural
institution's wayfinding does: tells you where you are without personality
getting in the way. Its width axis is a real display instrument.

**Newsreader** is a variable serif with an expressive italic. At 20px in the
dark it reads as **a subtitle in a film** or **a programme note in your hands** —
both exactly right. It is the whisper voice this concept has been describing
without ever naming a face that could deliver it.

This pairing is what performing-arts institutions look like. It is not what
software looks like.

**Accept it, refine it, or argue us out of it** — but the current pairing reads
as a product interface, and this is a theatre.

## The scale, and the law of one shout

> **The typographic idea is the distance between a whisper and a shout — and how
> rarely we shout.**

| Role | Face | Size | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Whisper** | Newsreader *italic*, light | 20px | ~12 times |
| **Statement** | Archivo, semibold | 40px | **2 times** |
| **THE SHOUT** | Archivo, bold, width 112 | 180px | **once — on the entire page** |
| **Programme** | Newsreader roman | 17px | throughout |
| **Label** | Archivo, uppercase, tracked | 12px | sparingly |

**The current build sets display type in the hero and four other places. Five
shouts is no shouts.** One word on the whole homepage is set large, and it lands
because nothing else competes with it.

## Rules

- **Nothing is centred until the house lights come up.** The film is hung left,
  like a subtitle.
- **Never more than eight words in frame** during the dark movements.
- **Measure 62–68 characters** for all reading copy. Never full-bleed text.
- **Italic is a voice, not an emphasis.** It marks the child's interior line.
  Never use it for stress inside a sentence.
- **Tracking applies to uppercase labels only.** No letterspaced lowercase.
- **Body text is never smaller than 16px, on any device.**

⚠️ The shout word itself is **unwritten**. It is the largest type on the site and
needs a decision from the owner, not from design.

# 11. Colour direction

## Colour is an arc, not a palette

Most websites have a palette. **This one has a temperature that changes as you
walk.**

The decisive idea: **the neutrals carry the story.** Backstage is blue-biased
black — cold, nervous, *before*. Memory is brown-biased black — warm, *past*.
The house is ivory — bright, ordinary, *after*.

Nobody will name these. Everybody will feel that the past is a different
temperature from the stage.

| Ground | Value | Bias | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pitch** | `#05070B` | blue-black | The opening. Absence. |
| **Wing** | `#0E121B` | the first blue | The walk |
| **Flash** | `#FFF3DC` | warm white | **The note. 400 milliseconds.** |
| **Memory** | `#17120C` | brown-black | Looking back |
| **Stage** | `#0D1220` | stage blue | The promise |
| **House** | `#F7F4EE` | warm ivory | The decision, the footer |
| **Spot** | `#E9A23B` | stage amber | The ration — see below |
| **Ash** | `#8A8578` | warm grey | Secondary text on dark |

Warm-biased neutrals are deliberate: **cool greys fight the amber and make
photography look clinical.**

## Amber is light, not decoration

> ## Amber appears four times on the entire homepage.

1. The seam of light under the door
2. The source that lights the walk
3. The flash at the moment of release
4. The primary call to action

**That is the whole budget.** It is not a border colour. Not a hover state. Not
an icon tint. Not a chip. Not a underline.

The current palette calls its accent "Spotlight" and then spends it like a brand
colour — everywhere, on everything. When the light is everywhere, none of it
reads as light, and the four moments that should be *events* become just more
amber.

## Contrast is a floor, not a target

Body text clears **4.5:1** minimum, higher where achievable. Large text and
interface elements clear **3:1**.

**The known trap: amber as text on a light ground fails.** It works as a *fill
with dark text* — that is how the current primary action reaches 9.7:1. Never
set amber type on ivory.

Deliver a computed contrast table for every text-and-ground pair you specify.
The previous design system published ratios that did not survive computation;
two values had to be corrected after the fact. **Compute, do not estimate.**

# 12. Composition laws

The prohibitions in this brief are **compositional, not chromatic.** A dark page
with beautiful typography still looks like a template if it is built from a grid
of rounded cards.

These are structural bans. Each one is checkable in a review.

| Law | Why |
|---|---|
| **Border radius is zero.** One exception: the primary action, a full pill. | Radius is the single strongest component-library tell there is. |
| **No drop shadows. Ever.** | Depth comes only from the light source. A shadow that does not agree with the light is a lie about the room. |
| **No cards.** No bordered, filled or elevated content containers. | This is the "grid of rounded cards" prohibition, made enforceable. |
| **No generic icons.** | Icon rows are the most template-coded element on the web. |
| **Asymmetric measures.** Content occupies 5–7 of 12 columns, offset from centre. | Centred columns read as a document template. |
| **Movements are separated by full-bleed temperature shifts** — never by rules, never by cards. | The page changes *rooms*. It does not stack *sections*. |
| **One idea per screen** during the dark movements. | Density is the enemy of anticipation. |
| **Letterbox 2.39:1 through the film**, opening at the moment of release. | Establishes that you are watching — then that you have arrived. |

An earlier pass already removed forty-seven rounded cards and twenty-four
shadows from this page. **These laws exist to stop them coming back.**

## Light and grain

**One light source.** It has a position, an intensity and a temperature.
**Everything is lit by it** — the floor, the figure, the texture, and the type
itself. Glyph brightness shifts with the source, so words sit *in the room*
rather than *on the glass*. Nobody will consciously notice. Everybody will feel it.

**One grain layer at 3% opacity over everything, including type.** It is the
cheapest item in this brief and the largest single contributor to "this feels
like film." Without it, dark gradients band and the page reads digital.

# 13. Motion philosophy

> **Motion here is camera work, not decoration.**

The page is not a document with animations applied. It is a continuous space
that a camera moves through, lit by a single source. Every movement is either
the camera or the light. Nothing moves because movement is nice.

## Principles

1. **The scroll is footsteps.** It converts a user-interface gesture into a
   physical act. The entire concept lives here.
2. **One light, one physics.** Every element obeys the same source. A single
   unifying logic is what makes a page feel like a *place*.
3. **Stillness is a device.** The most powerful moment in the sequence is 1.5
   seconds in which absolutely nothing moves. Nerve, as design.
4. **The release must be earned.** Four movements of darkness pay for 400
   milliseconds of warm white. Shorten the dark and the light means nothing.
5. **Never animate a number someone is about to act on.** Prices, deposits,
   refund terms, dates, capacity, guarantee text and contact details are always
   still, always legible, never revealed.

## The forbidden vocabulary

> ❌ **Fade up. Slide up. Stagger.**

Banned by name. They are the default vocabulary of every template on the web.

**If an element must appear, it is revealed by light, by mask, or by the camera
moving** — never by translating upward into opacity.

Also prohibited: custom cursors · cursor followers · loading percentages ·
scroll-jacking · forced snapping · horizontal scroll · parallax on text ·
auto-advancing carousels · typewriter headings · text scramble · confetti and
sparkle particles · a sticky call to action during the film *(a salesman at a
funeral)*.

**There is no preloader.** The dark opening **is** the load screen: black, one
line of type, buying a legitimate second of loading time while looking like art
direction.

## Restraint is the budget

Motion intensity is **inversely proportional to how close the visitor is to a
decision.** The homepage and the flagship programme run high. Pricing and
scholarship pages run minimal. Contact and FAQ pages do not move at all.

**A maximum of two pinned sequences exist across the entire site.**

## Reduced motion is a parallel cut, not a degraded one

Some visitors will disable motion — by system preference or by choice. They must
receive **the same story, the same colour arc, the same emotional shape**,
delivered without movement.

**Every scroll-driven moment needs a static equivalent that carries the same
meaning.** If a moment is unintelligible without motion, it is not finished.

An honest inventory, because it determines the entire visual strategy.

| | |
|---|---|
| Genuine photographs of this business | **18** |
| Photographs cleared for use | **0** |
| Videos | **0** |
| Downloadable documents | **0** |
| Stock or template images | 26 |

**All eighteen genuine photographs show identifiable children.** No photo-release

unconfirmed. **None may appear in any comp, mockup or presentation. They should
not circulate at all while consent is open.**

**There is no video** — for a business whose entire promise is live performance.
This is the largest gap in the media library and the most valuable thing the
owner could commission.

**The logo exists only as a raster file.** It also serves as the social sharing
image for every page, so a share of any page shows only the logo, never relevant
imagery.

## What this means

**Typography and light must carry the page.** That is the honest starting point,
and it is why sections 10–12 are the heart of this brief.

**And a photo shoot built on "you never see a face" needs no consent forms.**
Backs, hands, silhouettes, shoulders, shadows, empty stages, an audience as dark
shapes. That is the fastest route to authentic imagery this business has, and a
shot list for it is a deliverable of this project.

## Treatment for every image, photographed or generated

- **Desaturate to roughly 35%**, then re-tint toward the temperature of the
  movement it sits in. No image keeps its native colour; every image belongs to
  the room it is in.
- **Crush the blacks slightly.** Lift nothing. Detail lives in the highlights.
- **Vignette consistent with the light source.**
- **Grain applied after tinting**, so it sits *in* the image rather than *on* it.
- **Aspect ratios: 2.39:1 in the film movements, 4:5 or 3:4 at the desk.** Never
  16:9 — it reads as video-player furniture.

# 16. Things the designer MUST NOT invent

This is the hard constraint of the project, and it is not stylistic. The business
has seventeen unresolved factual conflicts and real exposure on several of them.

> ❌ **Any price.** Only the $25 spot-hold and the camp's $450 / $400 are
> published. Tuition for every programme, including the flagship, is unknown.
>
> ❌ **Any teacher.** No name, no face, no biography, no credential, no portrait.
> The site names no instructor anywhere; only customer reviews do.
>
> ❌ **Any testimonial**, or any quote attributed to a real person. Fourteen real
> ones exist. Three of them name children, and need consent before use.
>
> ❌ **Any photograph of a student, classroom, facility or performance.**
>
> ❌ **Any showcase date, event, or award.** No event record exists.
>
> ❌ **Student counts, years in business, success rates, "500+ families served."**
> None is published anywhere.
>
> ❌ **Star ratings or an aggregate review score.** The site displays none.
>
> ❌ **A Spanish page or a language switcher** as though it exists today.
>
> ❌ **Social media profiles, handles or follower counts.** None exist.
>
> ❌ **The words "every student."**

**Where a fallback is documented, the fallback is the design.** Do not solve a
constraint by supplying the thing the constraint prohibits.

# 17. Things the designer SHOULD redesign

Everything visual. Specifically:

**The hero.** It is currently a poster — a smart reference *to* theatre rather
than an experience *of* it. A poster on a wall has never made anyone cry. The
curtain going up has.

**The 90-Day Journey.** Currently rendered as a dot travelling a rule: a
progress bar with mood lighting. Abstraction has been applied to the least
abstract thing imaginable — a frightened child walking into light. **This is the
signature moment of the entire site and it is the weakest thing on it.**

**The typographic system.** See section 10. The type currently does no
emotional work.

**The use of colour.** The accent behaves like a brand colour rather than like
light.

**The composition.** Still symmetric, still centred, still stacked. It reads as
sections assembled rather than rooms walked through.

**The trial offer's presentation.** The $25 and its credited-back term are the
strongest commercial asset on the site and are nearly invisible.

**The scholarship section.** The families who most need this route currently get
the least help finding it.

**The performance evidence.** It must carry conviction without a single
photograph.

**The footer.** Currently an afterthought; it is where a parent goes to find the
address and the hours, and it is the last thing they see.

# 20. Required motion storyboard

Not engineering — direction. What the film looks like, described so it can be
built and then verified.

**Deliver a shot-by-shot storyboard covering:**

1. **The opening.** What the visitor sees in the first 1.2 seconds, before
   anything loads. What the light is doing before they touch anything.
2. **The walk.** How the light travels, at what pace, from where to where. What
   the ground does. What the camera does — push, pull, or hold.
3. **The mark.** The stillness. Its exact length and what precedes and follows
   it. Why the visitor should feel nervous.
4. **The release.** The single most important 400 milliseconds in the project.
   What flashes, what opens, what changes temperature, and what the visitor is
   left looking at.
5. **The house lights.** How the page comes up out of the dark into the ivory
   where the decision is made. Physical and slow, never a cut.
6. **The close.** How the film ends and the ordinary, competent, trustworthy
   part of the page begins.

**For each beat, state:** what the light does · what the camera does · what the
type does · what the visitor should feel · **and how the same beat reads with
motion disabled.**

**Also specify:** which single moment is the climax, and what makes it land.

# 21. Required implementation handoff

The build team can execute a visual language quickly **if it arrives as named
values rather than as pictures.** The architecture was built to be re-skinned
from a central set of definitions — colour, type, spacing, radius, elevation.
A direction expressed that way propagates everywhere without rebuilding
components. A direction expressed only as flat images requires someone to guess.

## Deliver

**A. Typography**
Every role named, with face, size, weight, width, tracking and leading. Fluid
behaviour between 375px and 1440px. Specimens at real rendered size, **in
English and in Spanish**. A stated rule for how often large type may appear.

**B. Colour**
The full palette as named values with hex. A semantic map: page ground, raised
surface, body text, muted text, link, primary action, focus indicator. **A
computed contrast table for every text-and-ground pair, with ratios.** A stated
rule for where the accent may and may not be used.

**C. Composition**
Grid, columns, gutters, container widths. Radius, border and elevation rules,
including which are zero. Spacing scale and vertical rhythm. How movements are
separated. Behaviour at 375, 768, 1024 and 1440.

**D. Art direction, movement by movement**
For each of the twelve movements: ground temperature, type roles used,
composition and alignment, imagery or texture, what the light is doing, and what
it must make the visitor feel.

**E. Imagery**
A shot list for a photography brief that honours *you never see a face*. A
treatment specification — grade, saturation, tinting, grain, vignette, aspect
ratios. A verdict on each of the eight generated assets. A recommendation on
video.

**F. Identity**
An assessment of the existing logo against the new palette, and a wordmark
treatment. Favicon and social-sharing image direction.

**G. Interface elements**
Visual treatment for actions (primary, secondary, tertiary; four sizes), links,
form fields, labels, error states, navigation in both states, and the footer.
Every control clears 44 × 44px.

**H. Motion**
The storyboard from section 20, plus the reduced-motion parallel cut.

## Every rule arrives with its reason

A value without a rationale cannot be defended in review, and will be eroded by
the first person who finds it inconvenient. **State why**, in a sentence.

# The test

> ## A parent scrolling on a phone in a school pickup line feels anticipation before they have read a single benefit — and a fifteen-year-old does not feel it was made for a child.

## It has failed if

- **Amber becomes an interface accent.** The four moments stop being events.
- **A second shout appears.** The first one stops mattering.
- **A card appears.** The whole prohibition list collapses at once.
- **The grain is cut to save weight.** It is 3% opacity and it is load-bearing.
- **The dark gets shortened** because it feels slow. The dark is what the light
  is paid for.
- **Something gets invented** because the true version was less convenient.
