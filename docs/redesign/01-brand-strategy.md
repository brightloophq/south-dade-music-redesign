# 01 — Brand Strategy

**Project:** South Dade Music redesign
**Phase:** 3 — Product Strategy
**Date:** 2026-08-05
**Source of truth:** `docs/source-content/` (Phase 2 extraction). No business facts are invented here. Anything unverified is marked ⚠️ **UNCONFIRMED** and carries a decision gate.

---

## 0. The strategic insight

The Phase 2 extraction surfaced one thing that changes everything about this project:

> **South Dade Music already owns a genuinely differentiated product — and sells it like a commodity.**

The academy has a named, structured, guaranteed transformation programme:

- The **90-Day Stage Program™** — a defined cycle ending in a live showcase
- The **Stage-Ready Guarantee** — *"if your child is not prepared to step on stage at the end of the 90-day cycle, we will continue coaching them at no additional charge until they are ready"*
- The **Gradual Exposure Ladder** — a named method for moving anxious children toward performance
- A **two-track weekly structure** — *"one for skill, one for band application"*
- Real showcase photography, 14 authentic testimonials, and Step Up for Students scholarship access

Almost none of that is doing any work on the current site. The homepage hero is followed by keyword copy: *"Music Lessons Near Me"*, *"Kids Music Classes South Dade"*, *"Piano Lessons Near Me"*. The flagship programme page has **no price, no testimonial, no showcase photo, and no link to the two classes it is made of**. The guarantee appears on two pages out of twenty-six. The word "trial" appears thirty times without terms.

The current site sells **lessons**. The business delivers **transformation**.

**The entire redesign is the work of closing that gap.**

## 1. Mission

> **We turn nervous beginners into confident performers — in ninety days, on a real stage, in front of the people who love them.**

**Grounded in source:** `/about/` — *"South Dade Music was created with a simple mission to make music education accessible and meaningful for every student."* · `/resources/` — *"No. This is a performance program."* · Footer, every page — *"where every student performs in a live showcase within 90 days."*

**What changed:** the extracted mission statement is SEO copy — *"provide simple and clear music education for families searching for Music Lessons Near Me and trusted Kids Music Classes South Dade."* Keyword phrases are embedded directly into the mission sentence. That is not a mission; it is a meta description that escaped. The mission above says the same thing the business actually does, without the keyword freight.

## 2. Vision

> **To be the reason South Miami-Dade has a generation of kids who aren't afraid of a room full of people.**

**Grounded in source:** `/about/` — *"Our vision is to build a strong music community where every student has the opportunity to learn, perform, and grow."* · homepage — *"Because once they realize they can get through something that feels scary, it changes how they approach everything else."*

That second line, buried in the homepage's four-pillar block, is the single best sentence on the current website. It is the vision. It should be treated as protected copy and given a prominent home in the redesign.

## 3. Brand personality

Five traits, each with a behavioural rule and an anti-pattern. These govern copy, design, and motion.

| Trait | Means | Does not mean | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Confident** | We state what happens and when. "Week 12: live showcase." | Boastful, superlative-stacking, "best in Florida" | Could a parent diary it? |
| **Warm** | Adults who like children and say so plainly. | Cutesy, cartoonish, primary-colour "kids' club" | Would a 15-year-old be embarrassed? |
| **Rigorous** | A named method, a real structure, honest standards. | Cold, conservatory-elitist, gatekeeping | Does it respect a total beginner? |
| **Local** | Florida City, Homestead, Cutler Bay, Palmetto Bay — by name. | Generic "South Dade" filler, near-me keyword mush | Would a neighbour recognise it? |
| **Bilingual by default** | Spanish is a first-class path, not a footnote. | A translate widget bolted on | Can a Spanish-only parent enrol unaided? |

### Personality dials

```
Formal        ·······●··   Familiar
Playful       ····●·····   Serious
Loud          ······●···   Composed
Aspirational  ········●·   Accessible
Cool          ·····●····   Warm
```

The brand sits **familiar, composed, accessible and warm** — a professional academy that talks to you like a neighbour, not an institution and not a birthday-party entertainer.

## 4. Brand voice

### Principles

1. **Say the number.** "12 weeks." "Ages 7–15." "Two classes a week." Vague is the enemy of trust with parents.
2. **Lead with the child, not the school.** "Your daughter will play her first song by week three" beats "We offer structured piano instruction."
3. **Name the fear.** Parents of shy kids are the core market. The current FAQ already does this well: *"What if my child is too shy?" — "That is exactly what we train for."* Keep this register.
4. **Never keyword-stuff.** Phase 2 found *"Singing Lessons For Kids"* six times on one page and *"Group Music Classes South Dade"* used as a noun inside body sentences. Banned outright. SEO is earned by structure and depth (see `06-seo-strategy.md`), not repetition.
5. **Short sentences carry weight.** The best extracted line is eighteen words long.
6. **Write Spanish, don't translate it.** Copy is authored for both languages; idiom is not machine-carried across.

### Voice by surface

| Surface | Register | Example direction |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Declarative, one promise | "In 90 days, your child walks on stage." |
| Programme pages | Structural, specific, week-by-week | "Weeks 1–10 build the skill. Week 11 they play for the class. Week 12 they play for you." |
| Instrument pages | Practical, parent-answering | "Most kids start piano around 5 or 6. A keyboard is enough to begin." |
| Scholarship | Plain, procedural, dignified | "Step Up covers this. Here's how to apply. It takes about twenty minutes." |
| Camp | Urgent but honest | "60 seats. Two sessions. When they're gone, they're gone." |
| Testimonials | Untouched | Never edit a parent's words. Verbatim or omit. |
| Legal / policy | Unambiguous | No softening of refund or deposit terms. |

### Words we use / avoid

**Use:** stage · showcase · confidence · ensemble · ready · perform · week · coach · together
**Avoid:** cheap · affordable · classes near me · world-class · state-of-the-art · unlock your potential · passionate about music (unearned) · journey (overused in current copy)

⚠️ **UNCONFIRMED — voice constraint:** Two testimonials on `try.southdademusic.com` are explicitly religious (*"Grateful to God…"*, *"worshiping the Lord"*), implying a faith-linked performance context that appears nowhere on the main site. **Decision gate B-1:** is the academy faith-affiliated, faith-adjacent, or secular? This changes hero copy, event framing, and which testimonials can be featured.

## 5. Target audience

Derived from extracted facts only: ages 3–18 primary focus with adult provision; a "large portion" of families Spanish-speaking; Step Up PEP and UA scholarship families; service area Florida City, Homestead, Cutler Bay, Palmetto Bay.

### Primary — "The Confidence Parent" (~55% of demand)

Parent of a 6–12-year-old in South Miami-Dade. The child is bright and shy, or bright and scattered. The parent is not shopping for a musician; they are shopping for a **change in their kid**.

- **Trigger:** a teacher comment, a school talent show, a cousin who plays, a child who won't speak up
- **Buying question:** *"Will this actually change how my child carries themselves?"*
- **Objection:** *"We tried an activity before and they quit after a month."*
- **What converts them:** the 90-day structure, the guarantee, and showcase photographs of kids who look like their kid
- **What loses them:** no price, no teacher faces, no evidence a showcase ever happened — all three are currently true

### Secondary — "The Scholarship Family" (high volume, underserved)

A Florida family holding or eligible for **Step Up PEP** or **Unique Abilities (UA)** funding. Frequently Spanish-speaking. Often navigating a special-needs pathway.

- **Buying question:** *"Do you take my scholarship, and what do I actually have to do?"*
- **Objection:** *"Will my child be put in a separate, lesser group?"*
- **Current failure:** `/step-up-accessibility/` has **no eligibility criteria, no application steps, no document list, and no link to Step Up for Students.** It also drops the legal disclaimer that the `/resources/` FAQ carries. This is the single largest untapped demand pool on the site.
- **What converts them:** a step-by-step application path, in Spanish, with the inclusion promise stated plainly — *"All students learn together in the same supportive environment"* (already extracted, already good)

### Tertiary — "The Camp Parent" (seasonal, transactional, high intent)

Needs childcare that isn't screen time, June–July, ages 7–15.

- **Buying question:** *"What are the dates, what does it cost, and is my kid safe?"*
- **Objection:** the deposit is non-refundable and **its amount is not published anywhere**
- **What converts them:** dates, price, capacity, a real day-in-the-life, staff visibility, and clear refund terms *before* the form

### Quaternary — "The Teen" (influencer, not buyer)

13–17. Chooses guitar, drums, bass, or voice. Cares about playing in a band and not being embarrassed. Will veto anything that looks like a children's website.

- **Converts on:** Band Builders, ensemble footage, the idea of a real stage
- **Design implication:** the visual system must carry teen credibility without alienating a 6-year-old's parent — this is the hardest single constraint in `04-design-system.md`

### Quinary — "The Returning Adult" (small volume, high margin, high advocacy)

Extracted evidence is thin: `/programs/` has an "Adults" segment, `/private-lessons/` says *"kids, teens, and adults,"* and the subdomain says *"picking it back up as an adult."* No adult-specific page exists.

⚠️ **Decision gate B-2:** is adult provision a real, staffed offer or legacy copy? If real, it needs its own page; if not, it should be removed rather than half-promised.

## 6. Parent emotional journey

Six stages. Each maps to a page, an emotion, and a single job.

| Stage | Feeling | Question in their head | Page that must answer it | Job of that page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Ache** | Quiet worry | *"She's so talented but she won't put her hand up."* | Homepage hero | Name the feeling in one line |
| **2 · Hope** | Cautious interest | *"Wait — they promise a stage in 90 days?"* | Homepage → `/programs/90-day-stage-program` | Make the promise concrete and dated |
| **3 · Scrutiny** | Sceptical | *"Who are these people? Is this real?"* | `/about`, `/teachers`, `/performances` | Show faces, credentials, and proof it happened |
| **4 · Arithmetic** | Practical | *"What does it cost, when, and can I get there?"* | `/pricing`, `/contact` | Remove every unknown |
| **5 · Commitment** | Nervous excitement | *"Okay. How do we start?"* | Trial booking | One step, no surprises, price disclosed |
| **6 · Pride** | Belonging | *"That's my kid up there."* | Showcase, `/performances` | Convert a customer into an advocate |

**The current site fails at stages 3, 4 and 5.** Stage 3: not one instructor is named anywhere except inside customer reviews. Stage 4: no price exists for any lesson product. Stage 5: thirty CTAs point at one undifferentiated widget, and the $25 charge is disclosed on two pages out of twenty-six.

## 7. Student emotional journey

This maps directly onto the **Gradual Exposure Ladder** already named in the extraction. The redesign should make the ladder visible — it is the most persuasive asset the business owns and it is currently one sentence in an FAQ.

| Rung | Where the student is | Emotional state | Site expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Alone** | First lesson, one room, one teacher | Self-conscious | "You start in a room with one person who's on your side." |
| **2 · Heard** | Plays a full piece for the teacher | Small pride | First-song milestone |
| **3 · Beside** | Band Builders — plays alongside peers | Safety in numbers | Ensemble imagery |
| **4 · Among** | Week 11 — plays for the class | Manageable exposure | The rehearsal step, named |
| **5 · Before** | Week 12 — dress rehearsal and live showcase | Fear converted to adrenaline | Showcase film and photography |
| **6 · Again** | Asks when the next one is | Identity shift: *"I'm someone who performs"* | Alumni and repeat-cycle story |

⚠️ **UNCONFIRMED:** the extraction gives only the name "Gradual Exposure Ladder" and *"move them from isolation to community safely."* The six rungs above are a reasonable reconstruction from the week structure (Weeks 1–10 / Week 11 peer / Week 12 showcase) but **must be confirmed with the owner before publication as method.** **Decision gate B-3.**

## 8. Unique Selling Proposition

### Primary USP

> **The only music academy in South Miami-Dade that puts your child on a stage within 90 days — and keeps coaching for free until they're ready.**

Three components, all extracted, none invented:
1. **A deadline** — 90 days / 12 weeks, ending in a live showcase
2. **A guarantee** — free continued coaching if not stage-ready
3. **A method** — Gradual Exposure Ladder, two classes weekly (skill + band application)

### Why this is defensible

Independent music teachers and franchise lesson chains sell **time** (a 30-minute slot, weekly, indefinitely). This academy sells an **outcome with a date**. That is a categorically different purchase, and it is very hard for a solo teacher to copy because it requires an ensemble programme, a venue, and a recurring showcase calendar.

### ⚠️ The USP has an integrity problem that must be fixed first

Phase 2 conflict #10. Three claims are live simultaneously and cannot all be true:

- Footer, every page: *"every student performs in a live showcase within 90 days"* — **unconditional**
- `/90-day-stage-program/`: students *"get the chance to perform"* — **conditional**
- The guarantee itself: *"if your child is **not prepared** to step on stage at the end of the 90-day cycle…"* — **concedes the unconditional claim is false**

**Recommended resolution — honest and stronger than the current claim:**

> **"Every student gets stage-ready. Most in 90 days. However long it takes yours, we keep coaching — at no extra cost."**

This keeps the deadline, keeps the guarantee, removes the falsifiable absolute, and turns the guarantee from fine print into the proof point. **Decision gate B-4 — owner must approve the wording change.** Until approved, the unconditional footer line must not be carried into the new build.

## 9. Brand differentiation

| Dimension | Typical local competitor | South Dade Music (post-redesign) |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit sold** | A weekly 30-minute slot | A 90-day transformation cycle |
| **Proof offered** | "Experienced teachers" | Dated showcases, film, named coaches |
| **Risk carried by** | The parent | The academy (Stage-Ready Guarantee) |
| **Group vs private** | One or the other | Both, deliberately paired — skill + band |
| **Performance** | Optional annual recital | Structural, every cycle, week 12 |
| **Scholarships** | Rarely handled | Step Up PEP + UA, guided application |
| **Language** | English, maybe a translate widget | Authored bilingual |
| **Youngest served** | Usually 6+ | 3+ (Early Childhood) |
| **Community** | Transactional | *"Community driven, not transactional"* (their own words, `/about/`) |

### The three moats

1. **The guarantee.** Nobody else in a price-sensitive local market will underwrite the outcome.
2. **The ladder.** A named method for shy children is a product, not a feature.
3. **Bilingual + scholarship fluency.** In this specific service area this is a structural advantage, currently claimed and undelivered.

## 10. Competitive positioning

### Positioning statement

> For **South Miami-Dade parents who want their child to grow in confidence, not just learn an instrument**, South Dade Music is **the performance academy that puts every student on a real stage within 90 days**, because **we teach skill and ensemble in parallel and guarantee stage-readiness** — unlike **lesson providers who sell time by the half-hour and hope a recital happens.**

### The map

Two axes that matter to this buyer: **Outcome certainty** (does anything specific happen, by when?) and **Warmth** (does my child belong here?).

```
                    HIGH OUTCOME CERTAINTY
                              │
       Conservatory /         │        ★ SOUTH DADE MUSIC
       exam-track studios     │          (target position)
       (certain, cold)        │        (certain, warm)
                              │
    COLD ─────────────────────┼───────────────────────── WARM
                              │
       Big-box franchise      │        Friendly independent
       lesson chains          │        teacher / church group
       (vague, impersonal)    │        (warm, no structure)
                              │
                     LOW OUTCOME CERTAINTY
```

The upper-right quadrant is empty in this market and the business already has the product to own it. **The current website places the brand in the lower-left.**

### Category we are competing against (real)

Not other music schools. The real competition for a Tuesday afternoon and $150–250 a month is: **soccer, dance, jiu-jitsu, Kumon, and the iPad.** Every one of those sells a visible outcome — a belt, a recital, a grade level, a game on Saturday. Music lessons traditionally sell nothing visible for two years. **The 90-day showcase is how this brand competes with a black belt.** That framing should drive homepage copy.

## 12. The transformation story

The narrative spine of the homepage and the `/programs/90-day-stage-program` page. Six beats.

**Beat 1 — Recognition.**
> *"She sings constantly at home. In front of anyone else, nothing."*
Open with the parent's actual experience. Not with the school.

**Beat 2 — Reframe.**
> *"That's not shyness. That's a skill she hasn't been taught yet."*
Moves the problem from the child's character to a teachable gap. This is the persuasive hinge.

**Beat 3 — Method.**
> *"We teach it the way you'd teach swimming — one safe step at a time."*
The Gradual Exposure Ladder, made visible: alone → heard → beside → among → before.

**Beat 4 — Structure.**
> *"Weeks 1 to 10, they build the skill. Week 11, they play for the class. Week 12, they play for you."*
Verbatim structure from the extraction. A parent can put this in a calendar.

**Beat 5 — Guarantee.**
> *"And if they're not ready at week 12, we keep coaching. No extra charge."*
De-risks the decision at exactly the moment doubt peaks.

**Beat 6 — Belonging.**
> *"Then they ask when the next one is."*
The outcome is not a performance. It is a child who now identifies as someone who performs.

**Existing protected line — must survive the rewrite:**
> *"Because once they realize they can get through something that feels scary, it changes how they approach everything else."*

## 13. Tagline recommendations

Evaluated against: memorability, ownership of the differentiator, bilingual portability, teen-safety, and whether it survives being said aloud by a parent.

### Recommended — primary

> ## Ready for the stage.

**Why:** Three words. Contains the promise (stage), the guarantee language (ready/stage-ready), and doubles as a status the child earns. Works as a lockup with the logo, as a showcase title card, and as a certificate line at week 12. Translates cleanly: **"Listos para el escenario."**

**Usage:** primary lockup, hero sign-off, showcase branding.

### Recommended — secondary / explanatory

> ## Every student, on a stage, within 90 days.

**Why:** Carries the full mechanic for cold traffic and paid ads. ⚠️ Must be reworded pending gate B-4 — suggested compliant form: **"On a stage in 90 days."**

### Alternates considered

| Tagline | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Build Community. Make Music.** | ✅ **Retain as an internal/community endline.** It is the existing extracted tagline (5 pages) and has equity — but it describes the school, not the child's outcome. Demote from primary. |
| **From the practice room to the spotlight.** | Good, but long; already used as an H2 on the programme page — keep it there. |
| **Confidence, in twelve weeks.** | Strong for the Confidence Parent; weak for teens. Use as a campaign line, not the master tagline. |
| **The stage is the syllabus.** | Memorable and true to the method. Slightly abstract for a Spanish-first parent. Hold as a brand-film line. |
| **Where shy kids become performers.** | Highest emotional resonance, but boxes out confident kids and teens. Use in targeted paid social only. |
| ~~"Music Lessons Near Me"~~ | Not a tagline. It is a search query currently occupying the mission statement. **Remove entirely.** |

### Lockup guidance

```
SOUTH DADE MUSIC
Ready for the stage.

  — or, for scholarship and community contexts —

SOUTH DADE MUSIC
Build Community. Make Music.
```

⚠️ **Decision gate B-5 — brand name.** The estate currently runs four names: *South Dade Music* (footer, sitewide), *South Dade Music Academy* (subdomain, 4 testimonials, lost events archive, `/singing-lessons/`), *SDMA*, and *South Dade Music LLC*. Taglines, logo lockups, structured data, and the ™ usage on "90-Day Stage Program" all block on this. **This is the first decision the owner must make.**

| Gate | Question | Blocks |
|---|---|---|
| **B-1** | Faith-affiliated, faith-adjacent, or secular? | Testimonial selection, event framing, imagery |

Gates **B-4, B-5, B-7 and B-8** are on the critical path to a homepage. See `10-launch-plan.md`.
