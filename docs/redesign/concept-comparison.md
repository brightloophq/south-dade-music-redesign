## 0. How these were judged

Three concepts, one product. The temptation in a comparison like this is to
score them on craft — which one *looks* best in a mood board. That is the wrong
question, because all three are executable to a high standard by a competent
team.

The right question is: **which one can actually be built, for this business, with
the assets that exist, and still win the parent?**

That reframing is decisive, and it comes from Phase 2 and 3 rather than from
taste. The relevant facts:

| Reality | Source | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **18 usable photographs**, all depicting identifiable minors, **all consent-blocked** | `09-image-strategy.md` §1, gate I-1 | Any concept that needs a lot of photography cannot launch |
| **Zero video across 26 pages** | `09-image-strategy.md` §11, gate M-3 | A cinematic concept has no footage |
| **Zero dated events**; both event URLs 404 | `02-IA` §1, gate I-4 | "Season" and "proof" devices are unbacked |
| **No price for any product** | `01` §15, gate B-8 | A pricing-led concept has an empty table |
| **No instructor named anywhere** | `01` §6, gate B-7 | A people-led concept has no people |
| The Exposure Ladder rungs are a **reconstruction** | `01` §7, gate B-3 | A method-led signature moment may not survive owner review |

**Every concept is blocked on something. They are not blocked equally, and the
differences are not marginal.** That is the single most important finding in
this comparison.

### Scoring model

Nine weighted criteria, 100 points total. Weights reflect what actually
determines success for *this* business, not general web-design virtue.

| Criterion | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Buildability today** | 15 | Can it launch with the assets and decisions that exist? |
| **Fit to product truth** | 15 | Does it express what the business actually sells? |
| **Conversion effectiveness** | 15 | Does it move a parent from Ache to Commitment (`01` §6)? |
| **Audience coverage** | 12 | Confidence Parent + Scholarship Family + Teen, together |
| **Accessibility** | 12 | A market requirement — the academy serves UA scholarship students |
| **Mobile & performance** | 10 | Where the decision is actually made |
| **Differentiation** | 10 | Memorability in a forgettable category |
| **Maintainability & longevity** | 6 | Can the business live with it for five years? |
| **Effort efficiency** | 5 | Build cost relative to value returned |

## 2. Rank 1 — Concept A · The Performing Arts House · **81.7**

### Why it ranks first

**It is the only concept whose greatest strength is also the answer to the
project's biggest constraint.**

The academy has 18 consent-blocked photographs, no video, and no dated events.
Concept B needs photography, teacher portraits and prices. Concept C needs a
film that does not exist. Concept A needs **type, light and one photograph** —
and a poster tradition has sold theatre for two centuries on exactly that
budget.

Three further reasons:

1. **It is the truest to the product.** `04-design-system.md` §0 had already
   arrived at the stage as the resolution to the 6-year-old/15-year-old tension
   before this phase began. Concept A does not invent a metaphor; it commits to
   the one the strategy already found and the business already sells.
2. **The register break is a genuinely good structural idea.** Dark means
   persuasion, light means facts you can act on. It gives the site an
   information architecture a parent can *feel*, and it structurally enforces
   `05-motion-system.md` §17 — motion and drama recede as the user nears a
   decision. It is the mechanism that stops the concept from being a mood piece.
3. **It covers all three audiences.** Theatre is credible to a teenager, warm to
   a parent, and — in its light register — plain enough for the Scholarship
   Family. No other single concept does all three.

### Why it does not score higher

Accessibility (6/10) is the real drag. Dark-dominant design invites
light-on-dark prose at length, which is measurably harder for dyslexic and
astigmatic readers, and the dark→light ground transition carries a
flash-threshold obligation. Both are solvable — the concept's own §14 solves
them — but they are ongoing costs, not one-time fixes.

Its floor is also the lowest of the three. One velvet gradient, one bulb
graphic, one centred serif and it is a community-theatre flyer. **It demands
art direction to be held all the way through build**, which is a delivery risk
as much as a design one.

## 3. Rank 2 — Concept B · The Modern Academy · **74.8**

### Why it ranks second, and why the gap is smaller than it looks

Concept B wins **five of nine criteria** — more than Concept A. It is the best
of the three at accessibility, mobile, performance, conversion mechanics,
maintainability and effort efficiency. On a pure engineering scorecard it would
win.

It ranks second for two reasons:

1. **It is the most blocked concept in practice.** Its spine is pricing (B-8),
   teachers (B-7), the Exposure Ladder (B-3) and dated showcases (I-4). Remove
   those and it is an elegant empty frame — and unlike Concept A, it has no
   atmospheric fallback to stand on while the gates close. A structure-led
   concept with no content is a worse launch than an atmosphere-led concept with
   no photographs.
2. **It sells the wrong thing.** `01-brand-strategy.md` §0 is unambiguous: *"the
   current site sells lessons; the business delivers transformation."* A
   syllabus, a price table and a comparison grid are the vocabulary of the
   commodity end of that gap. They are necessary — they are not sufficient, and
   alone they risk re-committing the original sin in a better-looking way.

There is also the teen veto. `01` §5 warns that the 13–17 segment *"will veto
anything that looks like a children's website."* Light, systematic and
institutional does not read as a children's website — it reads as **school**,
which is what a teenager is trying to escape on a Thursday evening.

### What it is unambiguously right about

Everything it says about the decision layer. The `/pricing` page, the guided
Step Up path, terms above the form, intent captured at source, tables that
become cards on mobile, published structure over adjectives — **these are not
optional and they are not stylistic.** They are the fixes for the specific
failures Phase 2 documented, and they must survive into the final direction
intact. They do.

## 4. Rank 3 — Concept C · Cinematic Storytelling · **56.6**

### Why it ranks third

**It cannot be built.** Not "is hard to build" — cannot.

- Zero video exists across 26 pages (`09-image-strategy.md` §11)
- All 18 photographs are consent-blocked (gate I-1)
- The showcase shoot cannot be *scheduled* until a showcase is scheduled (I-4)
- It needs purpose-shot cinematic frames that have never been captured

A concept whose defining material does not exist and cannot yet be commissioned
is not a launch candidate, however good it is. Its score of 2/10 on
buildability is not a criticism of the idea; it is a statement of fact about the
calendar.

Beyond the blocker, it carries the worst accessibility profile (two pinned
scrubbed sequences, text over imagery, a separately-designed reduced-motion
experience), the worst mobile experience on the device where the decision is
made, the highest cost (~24 weeks plus external film and photography
production), and it spends **both** of the two permitted sitewide pinned
sequences on the homepage — leaving the flagship programme page structurally
weaker than the homepage about its own product.

It also buries price, ages and scholarship steps behind a film, which is close
to hostile to the Scholarship Family — `01` §5's largest untapped segment.

### Why it still matters enormously

Concept C scores **10/10 on differentiation and 9/10 on fit to product truth**
— the highest single scores anywhere in this comparison. It is the only concept
that solves the actual sales obstacle, which is *imaginative*, not
informational: a parent cannot picture their shy child on a stage, and no table
will make them.

Its chapter structure is also the best articulation in this document of `01`
§12's six-beat narrative. **That structure should be designed now and built
later.** It is the phase-two evolution of whatever launches, and it becomes
available the moment a showcase is filmed.

| Question | A | B | C | Verdict |

| Can it launch with zero approved photographs? | ✅ Yes | ⚠️ Degraded | ❌ No | **A** |

| Best on a mid-range Android at 10pm? | ⚠️ | ✅ **Best** | ❌ Worst | **B** |
| Most memorable the next morning? | ✅ Strong | ❌ Weakest | ✅ **Best** | **C / A** |
| Best for long-form (FAQ, Step Up, terms)? | ⚠️ Needs light register | ✅ **Best** | ⚠️ Conventional layer | **B** |
| Lowest risk of executing badly? | ❌ Lowest floor | ✅ **Highest floor** | ❌ Fragile | **B** |

**The pattern is unmistakable.** A wins the *can we ship it* questions. B wins
the *decision layer and long-form* questions. C wins the *memorability and
emotion* questions.

**They are not competing for the same job.** That is the finding that produces
the final direction.

data tooling for motion and art direction. The real difference is not
engineering weeks — it is **content weeks**, and there A is dramatically cheaper.

## 7. What each concept contributes to the final direction

The ranking is not a knockout. It determines the **spine**, not the whole body.

| From | Contribution to the final direction | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | The stage as architecture · the dark persuasion register · poster typography with the variable width axis · the 90-Day Timeline as the signature moment · box-office conversion language · generated light textures as the primary decorative layer | **Spine — adopted whole** |
| **B** | The light decision register · the `/pricing` page · the published 12-week syllabus · the guided Step Up path · fact-carrying navigation · tables that become cards · caption-as-proof photography · long-form as a first-class surface · the accessibility baseline | **Decision layer — adopted whole** |
| **C** | The six-beat chapter structure as the homepage's narrative order · the warming arc, confined to the Timeline · the escape-hatch discipline · the showcase film as the phase-two centrepiece | **Narrative order now; full cinematic treatment deferred** |

**The synthesis has one organising idea:** the site has **two rooms** — a house
where the parent is persuaded, and a desk where they decide — and moving between
them is a designed, meaningful act rather than a stylistic inconsistency.

That is the subject of [`final-art-direction.md`](./final-art-direction.md).

## 8. Recommendation

> **Adopt Concept A as the spine. Adopt Concept B wholesale as the decision
> layer. Adopt Concept C's chapter order now and hold its cinematic treatment
> for phase two, gated on the showcase film.**

**Do not build any of the three as specified.**

- Concept A alone under-serves the Scholarship Family and long-form content.
- Concept B alone re-commits the original sin of selling lessons instead of
  transformation.
- Concept C alone cannot be built this year.

### If the owner overrules and wants one, unmodified

| If they choose | Say this |
|---|---|
| **A** | Viable. Insist on the light register for pricing, scholarships, FAQ and terms — without it, accessibility and conversion both suffer materially. |
| **B** | Viable but blocked. It cannot ship until gates B-3, B-7 and B-8 close. Get written commitment on those dates before starting. |
| **C** | Not viable for launch. Recommend it as the phase-two target and propose commissioning the showcase film now (`09-image-strategy.md` §11 ranks it the #1 missing asset in the project regardless of concept). |
